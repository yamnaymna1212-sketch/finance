# The Gold Mind — "Same Street" Format

Same paycheck. Same street. Same bills. One choice changes. Two lives split.

## What we take from the references (and what we don't)

Take (these are format ideas, free to use):
- Split screen: two identical houses side by side, two characters.
- One variable changes per video. Everything else is identical.
- Time passes on screen (year counter). The gap grows visibly.
- Visual metaphors: debt = red weight, savings = gold pile/jar.
- Short vertical video, hook in 1 second, no intro.

Do NOT copy:
- Their characters (gorilla, white stick figures), their art, their logo style.
- Their exact title line ("SAME PAY. SAME ...") — we use our own.
- Our look stays: black + gold, line-art pawns. Gold (the one who acts) vs Ash (the one who waits or chooses the other path).

Our title line: **"SAME STREET. TWO CHOICES."** with a small tag on top (RENT VS OWN, 401(k) VS CASH, etc.).

## Fairness rules (on screen, every video)

1. One variable only.
2. Show BOTH sides' costs, including the ugly ones (repairs, taxes, fees, stress, risk).
3. Assumptions card for 2 seconds: starting pay, return %, rates, years.
4. No cherry-picked periods. Use a long average, say "past results are not a promise".
5. Let the loser have one real win. (Ash is not stupid. Ash has a reason.)
6. Not financial advice line in caption.

## The "sacrifice and survive" angle

Your idea: one character sacrifices now (skips trips, drives the old car, rents small) and survives a real problem later (layoff, car repair, medical bill). The other lives better now and gets hit by the same problem with no cushion.

Rule: the SAME shock hits both. Then we show who absorbs it. This is the emotional engine. Keep it honest: show what the sacrifice cost (the missed trips, the stress) too.

## Case File 001 — "Same Street. Skip the Match."

Hook (0–2s, on screen + voice): "Same job. Same pay. One walked away from free money."

Assumptions card: Salary $60,000. Employee puts in 6% ($300/month). Employer matches 100% up to 4% ($200/month). 7% average yearly return, 30 years. Before taxes on withdrawal.

- Gold: takes the match. $300 from pay + $200 free = $500/month.
- Ash: skips the 401(k), invests $300/month alone in a regular brokerage.

Numbers (computed, monthly compounding):
- Gold after 10 years: ~$86,500
- Gold after 20 years: ~$260,000
- Gold after 30 years: ~$610,000
- Ash after 10 years: ~$51,900
- Ash after 20 years: ~$156,300
- Ash after 30 years: ~$366,000
- Gap at 30 years: ~$244,000. Same own money out of pocket ($300/month).

Fair-comparison honesty (put on screen):
- 401(k) money is taxed when you take it out. Ash's brokerage gains are taxed too, but Ash can reach the money any time. Gold pays a penalty before age 59½ (with exceptions).
- Ash's real win: flexibility. If a shock hits at year 5, Ash can use the cash. Gold usually can't, without a cost.
- Takeaway: take the match first, then keep an emergency fund. Both, in that order.

Shot plan (about 40s)
- 0–2s: Split street, two identical houses, year counter at 0. Hook text.
- 2–6s: Assumptions card.
- 6–14s: Gold's jar fills with $300 + gold coins falling from the employer ($200). Ash's jar: $300 only.
- 14–24s: Year counter jumps 10, 20, 30. Jars compare. Numbers pop.
- 24–32s: Shock at year 5 replay: Ash uses cash, Gold is stuck. Give Ash the win.
- 32–38s: Gold fix: match first, then emergency fund.
- 38–40s: Question for comments: "Would you skip the match for flexibility?"

## Note on Rent vs Buy

Do not publish a rent-vs-buy number until the model is built with: price, down payment, rate, property tax, insurance, maintenance (1%/year), closing and selling costs, rent growth, and the return on the down payment if invested. Results change a lot by city and rate. Show the break-even year, not a winner.
